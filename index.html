Product — QNyx

The secure transport layer for unreliable networks.

Most encrypted tunnels assume a stable connection — and fall apart when they don't get one. QNyx was designed for the opposite assumption: that links will flap, packets will arrive out of order, and the network underneath you will change without warning. Its philosophy is coherence through embracing disruption.

QNyx creates an encrypted tunnel between two points, protecting everything that flows through it — video feeds, sensor data, command traffic, business communications — no matter what kind of connection carries it. It doesn't care whether your data crosses a satellite link, a cellular network, a tactical radio, or a home fiber line. If it speaks internet, QNyx protects it.

Post-quantum from the foundation up

QNyx uses the same NIST-standardized post-quantum algorithms selected to protect against future quantum computers, paired with Xaoc's custom HF7 cipher for payload protection. Adversaries recording your encrypted traffic today, hoping to decrypt it later, come away with nothing.

Keys that rotate themselves — without dropping a beat

Session keys refresh automatically on a fixed schedule while traffic keeps flowing. No handshake pauses, no tunnel teardowns, no gaps. In testing, QNyx completed over 1000 in-line key rotations without losing a single packet.

Fast enough to forget it's there

Sustained high throughput with well under a millisecond of added latency on ordinary commodity hardware. Full 4K video streams through it in real time.

Small footprint, zero fuss

The entire product is a single lightweight binary, roughly the size of a photo. Clients need no manual network setup at all — addressing is assigned and secured automatically during connection.

Technology

Why "post-quantum" matters now — not later.

The quantum threat, in one paragraph

Today's standard encryption relies on math problems that classical computers can't solve in any useful timeframe. Quantum computers change that math. And adversaries aren't waiting: encrypted data is being harvested and stored today so it can be decrypted the moment quantum capability arrives. Anything with a long shelf life — designs, contracts, communications, identities — is already at risk. QNyx closes that window now.

Built differently

Conventional encrypted tunnels depend on carefully ordered counters and stable sessions; when packets arrive out of order or a link flaps, they can desynchronize or fail. QNyx's HF7 cipher takes a different approach: every packet is protected with fresh randomness, so there's no fragile counter to break and nothing to desynchronize. Disorder in the network simply doesn't matter.

Proven under scrutiny

  • Independent cryptanalytic audit. The HF7 cipher underwent four iterative rounds of independent expert cryptanalysis. Issues found in early revisions were remediated.
  • Full-implementation security audit of the complete QNyx product, currently being finalized by the same independent auditor.
  • Planned red-team exercise: a live penetration test against a real QNyx-protected camera feed, where attackers win by either disrupting the feed or viewing its contents.
